Research

Continuous ECG Monitoring with a Loose T‑Shirt

MobiDx 2026

Loose clothing is the most comfortable wearable, but it was long considered unusable for ECG because the fabric moves with the body. Here, the shirt's own weight presses a thin kirigami sensor against the back, sweat forms a gel-free anchor to the skin, and a hybrid electrode senses motion so it can be filtered out. In a pilot study, it captured 92.7% of full ECG waveforms at rest and 84.5% of heartbeats while walking.

Noninvasive Optical Glucose Sensing

Scientific Reports 2025 • Sensors 2023 • UbiComp 2022

A portable box that estimates glucose levels from how the palm rotates polarized light, with a light trap that rejects unwanted reflections. In a 363-day home study with 30 participants, run with the Barbara Davis Center for Diabetes, over 95% of predictions were clinically accurate or acceptable.

Computational Fabrics for Health

IMWUT 2025 • MobiSys 2024 • ICORR 2025

Sensors made from conductive fabric and built into everyday textiles. A pillowcase that tracks heart rate, breathing, sleep posture and movement through the night (I built the sensing prototype), Joey, which supports kangaroo mother care for newborns (I developed the Android app), and a fabric sensor for hand-muscle activity in rehabilitation (I advised on building the sensors).

This acupuncture simulator was developed base on Interventional Sonography Training System. Since acupuncturists perform various manipulations including twisting the needle to achieve a "deqi" response, we installed a rotary encoder at the needle and connected it to an arduino microcontroller to measure the needle's rotation.
